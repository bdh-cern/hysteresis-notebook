- Italicise or quote paper titles
- 'et al' should be italicised and dot after al.
- Need to capitalise relevant words in section headers, e.g. 2.2 'The History of Railway Safety'
- Structure of ideas can be strange, e.g. 2.1, the paragraphs are about:
	1. What this project was meant to be about
	2. The fact you couldn't really do this because of issues with the field - makes sense
	3. There is a certain paper from 2022 - how is this relevant to the above?
	4. This paper is speculative (should be the same paragraph as #3 so readers understand the relevance earlier)
	5. Peleska et al cite a press release. In what context do they cite this? The reference given is to the press release, not to Peleska's comments. Why is this person relevant?
	6. Your conclusions about automatic railway - how do these follow from your two examples?
	7. Back to Peleska - considering you already made your conclusions, why are you going back to them?
	8. There is another paper by Peleska
	9. The contents of this paper (should be the same paragraph as above, and probably before the conclusion). Also the chapter structure (which should be its own paragraph) 
- Same sort of thing in 2.2: paragraph 2 concludes with 'railway safety was not “solved” outright, but incrementally stabilised through hard-won lessons' then paragraph 3 opens with 'Railway safety evolved through iterative design responses to failure' which is basically the same thing. I think you need to merge a lot of your paragraphs when they discuss the same thing. My personal rule of thumb is that every paragraph should basically have its own unique topic with introduction and conclusion, and there should be no hanging questions by the end of a paragraph (latter not an issue with yours but I found it in Karo's thesis)
- Some paragraphs are way too short, e.g. 'Over time, safety responsibility shifted away from individual operators toward tightly constrained systems of procedures, interlocks, and institutional oversight, a shift that fundamentally shapes what “autonomy” can mean in a railway context.'
	- Should have e.g. this an an intro, then some commentary or explication on the repurcussions, then a conclusion as to what this means for your project or the field in a way that leads into the next paragraph 
- Section 2.3.2 seems very important, but it doesn't have any references. It's also quite generalising, so maybe you could instead have something like a table which categorised 4 or 5 documented protocols into this framework to show how they're basically the same?
- Some sections lack immediate context. For example, the point of 2.3.3 only becomes evident about half way through. In the first paragraph, I wasn't sure why you were discussing apathy or what it specifically referred to. I think you need to put a bit more emphasis on motivating these and specifically referring back to the title of the sections. Another example is 2.3.1, 'Illusions of Autonomy in Rail'. I would include a short thesis: 'Public discourse often suggests that railway autonomy is almost a reality, with different rail routes claiming to be autonomous. **However, this autonomy is almost entirely illusory**.'
